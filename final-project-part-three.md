# Changes made since Part II
Because my Part II was a lot more structured and figured out, the changes that I made were nothing major in terms of the narrative and the flow. The big change made was primarily just taking out a section. I had a completely different structure going from Part I to Part II, but due to needing a more polished version for my upcoming in-class presentation, I made sure it was not just a rough concept of what I had wanted. With that being said, I did utilize nearly all of the feedback that I received to improve my story. 

As mentioned, I completely omitted the portion and data that I had worked with on Tableau that showed the protected area by country. There simply wasn't enough of a story there, which is why I had reorganized how I set things up, but final confirmation from interviewee #2 made me permanently take it out and never look back. I'm glad, because it would've felt a little bit too forced if I kept it in. There was no way to use Shorthand as effectively with the smooth transitions if I had kept my original idea going. I also was able to add more transitions as a result of the feedback I received, as the overall theme itself kind of developed into a theme of transitions; not having them towards the end felt wrong.

Other than that, I made sure to remove much of the excess space I have the tendency of leaving in drafts, and cut down a few of my wordy sentences. I also added a few specific statistics to the causes section to give the reader a better sense of the magnitude of habitat loss and poaching, and made the call to action a slight bit more specific to giraffes. 

## The audience
The audience has stayed steadily the same through each iteration, as the groups chosen are in my opinion the most directly impactful to the cause. As mentioned in Part II, Niger's government was able to avert much of the poaching that was going on to begin the recovery of the subspecies of West African giraffe. Enforcement and consistent monitoring is the necessary approach, and both legislators and NGOs have a hand in that pot. I had considered something like donations to a specific giraffe conservation group, which also would have worked, but it doesn't fit as well with my narrative comparing the differing conservation approaches. Had I chosen this approach, I would have focused more on how negatively impacted most subspecies are, and barely mention or omit any of the success stories.

## Final design decisions
Going from Part I to Part II, I knew that I wanted to shift gears to make what I have flow a bit more. It was after having a few clever transition ideas, that I decided to lean into them. It is certainly unconventional to take that approach, but I knew they would be well-received, and they were. Everybody who I showed the story to was a fan of the giraph. I changed the set up to maximize on how I showed these particular transitions.

## AI acknowledgements
I mainly used AI to brainstorm ideas for inspiration on what direction to take in my project and for finding additional sources and the more nuanced academic literature. I also used it to help minimize some of my wordiness, and to navigate the many functions of Tableau. The giraph transition was also an image of a giraffe that I had generated with AI.

# Final thoughts
My biggest takeaway for the project is to let the story come to you; I started off this project really struggling to find a direction, but the direction doesn't have to be completely clear to start. Even with my chosen topic, I ended up taking a different approach than intended, and that worked out in my favor. The story became more compelling and focused on what was necessary. Getting feedback from a range of people was also a great way to cover all of the bases of the story.
